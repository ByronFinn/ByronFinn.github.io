# Trump vs the New NYC Mayor: Prelude to a Political Storm


### The President's Contradictory Posture

In public, President Trump has spent weeks deriding Zohran Mamdani as an extremist, a communist, and a threat to New York City. He has also insisted that he is "much better looking" than the 34-year-old Mamdani.

But in private, according to two people who disclosed the president's remarks on condition of anonymity, Trump has described the New York City mayor-elect as a talented politician — sharp-minded and quick with an argument.

For all that grudging admiration, the two men appear headed for a showdown — the young democratic socialist squaring off against a president who long ago sized him up as the perfect target. To Trump, the mayor-elect embodies the Democratic opposition; hours after Mamdani pulled off his seemingly impossible victory, the president called Democrats "crazy" — and "Mamdani, or whatever the hell his name is" just as bad.

### The Federal-City Money Game

Trump's aides and allies acknowledge that Mamdani and New York City are likely to become the president's next round of targets, though some caution that because Trump owns multiple real estate assets in New York, the city's economic prosperity is bound up with his own vested interests. On Wednesday, Trump even said he might "help him out a little," because he wants New York City to succeed.

Even so, the president has threatened to withhold federal funds, giving the city nothing "beyond the minimum required by law." In fact, apart from a few narrow exceptions, he has no authority to withhold funding Congress has already approved. (The administration previously tried to withhold federal funds from some cities over immigration policy, and repeatedly lost in court.)

The federal government provides New York City with dozens of grants, covering health care, transportation, law enforcement and more. If the administration withholds any one of those expected allocations, litigation is close to certain.

### The New Mayor Hits Back Hard

Mamdani, for his part, looks ready for the fight. In his victory speech he challenged Trump head-on, vowing to strike back against federal interference in New York's internal affairs.

"Donald Trump, I know you're watching, and I have a message for you," he taunted the TV-loving president. "Turn it up."

(White House press secretary Karoline Leavitt later confirmed that Trump had indeed been watching the speech.)

Mamdani says he will not be cowed by the president's threats, and that New York City will provide the playbook for defeating Trump and his political movement.

"Listen to me, President Trump," he said. "If you come after one of us, you will have to go through all of us."

To fight the Trump administration, Mamdani has little recourse beyond lawsuits. The mayor-elect has pledged to hire 200 more lawyers for the city's legal department — partly, precisely, to fend off what his campaign called "presidential abuse of power."

### The "Perfect Opponent" on the Political Chessboard

In his second term, Trump has repeatedly shown a willingness to use the powers of the federal government to settle scores with political enemies, at times even with eagerness. He has stripped away billions in federal grants, aiming squarely at Democratic-governed states and cities like New York City; he has sent the National Guard into Democratic cities against their will; and he has directed the Justice Department to prosecute political opponents, including New York's attorney general.

But some of the president's allies say privately that Mamdani's victory may actually be good for Trump, letting him reuse the long-standing routine he has deployed to demonize Democratic leaders — as with former House Speaker Nancy Pelosi, Representative Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez of New York, and the Democratic billionaire backer George Soros — and thereby advance his political agenda.

Ever since Mamdani's upset win in the Democratic primary, Trump and his allies have cast him as the face of the party's future, working to paint the Democrats as an extremist force. On Wednesday, when Trump attacked Democrats for allowing transgender women and girls to compete in women's sports, he lumped Mamdani into the same camp.

"He thinks it's great to have men playing in women's sports," Trump said.

Facing Trump's policies targeting transgender people, Mamdani has pledged to defend the rights of transgender New Yorkers and make New York a "sanctuary city" for LGBTQ residents. He has yet to take an explicit position on the athlete question; his spokesperson pointed only to his past remarks.

### The Balancing Act Between Cooperation and Confrontation

Mamdani calls himself a democratic socialist, not a communist. He seems unafraid of going toe-to-toe with the president, and on Wednesday morning he played down the criticism that his victory speech had deliberately provoked Trump.

"If the president is willing to work together and deliver on his campaign promises of lowering the price of groceries or the cost of living, I will work with him," the mayor-elect said in an NY1 interview. "But if he wants to come after the people of this city, then I will stand up and defend them every step of the way."

David Axelrod, the Democratic strategist and longtime adviser to President Obama, said some of Mamdani's remarks about Trump amounted to "unnecessary schoolyard taunting."

"I don't think there's much point in getting into online taunting matches with Trump, but when he has essentially declared war on this city, pushing back hard is necessary," he said. "The key question is: how do you challenge the president while still holding on to the idea of a mayor who serves the entire city?"

Trump is a New Yorker born and bred, and he has always paid close attention to the city's affairs. His advisers tried to intervene in the election to block Mamdani and failed; last year Trump campaigned for president in New York City repeatedly, even though the city's voters reliably back Democrats.

Which is why some of Trump's allies in New York are encouraging him to take a more polished approach to the city.

John Catsimatidis, the New York grocery and oil billionaire, said he has told the president not to withhold money that would help the people of New York, and that he sees no need for the president to send the military into the city; instead, he has suggested Trump have the federal government closely monitor all federal money allocated to New York City.

"They're not going to give him money and let him spend it any way he wants," he added. "I think the president will be watching closely, because he cares about New York."
